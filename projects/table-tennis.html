01 / PHYSICS CORE

Spin is part of the state—not a visual effect

The ball carries position, velocity, and a full 3D angular-velocity vector. Quadratic aerodynamic drag slows a 2.7 g ball realistically, while the Magnus term uses spin × velocity to produce topspin dip, backspin float, and sidespin curve without shot-specific special cases.

Flight

Drag + Magnus force

Flight is advanced at a high fixed rate using regulation dimensions and SI units.

Contact

Spin-coupled bounce

Coulomb-limited friction transfers energy between ball speed and angular velocity on table contact.

Gameplay

AI + practice modes

A single-player match and focused serve and feel tests support rapid tuning of control and physics.

Performance

Prediction without stalls

The hottest integration path uses scalar in-place updates to avoid allocation spikes during AI trajectory prediction.

02 / MULTIPLAYER

One authoritative court, two mirrored viewpoints

LAN clients send paddle input over UDP; the server owns the match, advances the simulation, and broadcasts snapshots at roughly 60 Hz. Both players use the same near-side camera and the far client transforms positions, velocity, spin, and paddle normals through a 180° court rotation.

03 / STATUS

A technical experiment, intentionally presented as one

The project already supports first-person control, an AI opponent, practice modes, scoring, sound, and LAN play. Its value here is the simulation and networking work—not a claim that it is a finished commercial game.
