Bite Point

A focused browser racing simulation built to feel good on keyboard and mouse.

01 / THE FEEL

Vehicle dynamics that stay independent of rendering

The simulation owns the car state and lap rules; Three.js only renders them. A fixed-step vehicle model drives steering, load transfer, grip, collisions, and timing consistently across hardware, while scalable graphics preserve the same underlying race.

Driving

Keyboard + mouse

Mouse steering, throttle, and braking were tuned around clean, repeatable control rather than controller emulation.

Feedback

Ghosts + sectors

Personal bests, delta timing, sector splits, and saved ghost paths make improvement visible lap by lap.

Rendering

Scalable Three.js

Multiple cameras, terrain, scenery, shadows, post-processing, and quality settings share one simulation state.

Content

Data-driven tracks

Circuits and barriers are loaded from track data, with four routes and two distinct handling setups available.

02 / TRUSTED RESULTS

The leaderboard does not trust the browser

Each submitted lap includes an input recording. The server reconstructs the circuit and physics ruleset, re-simulates the lap, and accepts the result only when the replay agrees. Supabase handles identity and PostgreSQL persistence; a local Fastify implementation keeps development portable.

03 / RESULT

Playable, deployed, and designed as a complete product

Bite Point runs directly in the browser and includes onboarding, settings, timing, records, ghosts, multiple circuits, two car behaviours, and a production deployment pipeline—not just a driving demo.
